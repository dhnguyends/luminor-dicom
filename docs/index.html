Open-source CT viewer

Built around how radiologists actually read.

Luminor puts the seven things a radiologist does all day at the tip of a key: scroll, window, reformat, measure, project, compare and report. Fast, local and calm on the eyes.

Get started Read the user guide

Seven features. One reading routine.

A radiologist reads 40 to 80 CT studies a day, and the same handful of actions make up nearly all of that work. Luminor was designed from that routine, starting with what a radiologist needs most.

01 · Scroll & cine

Scrolling is the reading.

In the reading room: most of a radiologist's time goes into scrolling through the stack, following a vessel, a bronchus or a nodule from image to image. If scrolling lags, nothing else matters.

  • The mouse wheel always scrolls, whatever tool is active. Shift moves five images at a time.
  • Cine playback at 2 to 40 images per second with Space.
  • Image number and slice position in millimetres always on screen.

02 · Window & level

Every tissue, one key away.

In the reading room: every CT is read at least three times, in lung, soft-tissue and bone windows, and the window is adjusted dozens of times per study.

  • Right-drag anywhere for window and level, with any tool selected.
  • Presets on 1–5: lung, mediastinum, bone, liver, brain. They also work on AZERTY keyboards.
  • Exact centre and width entry, and invert with I.

03 · Multiplanar reconstruction

Three planes, one point in space.

In the reading room: a finding is confirmed, measured and located in coronal and sagittal planes, not on axial images alone.

  • Coronal and sagittal views rebuilt with true physical proportions, head at the top.
  • Click with the crosshair to move all three planes to the same point.
  • Colour-coded planes and orientation letters calculated from the DICOM tags.
04 · Measurements

Numbers you can report.

In the reading room: nodule size drives follow-up recommendations, and a lesion's density tells cyst from fat, soft tissue or calcium.

  • Live Hounsfield value under the cursor, with patient coordinates.
  • Distances in millimetres from the true pixel spacing, including on reformats.
  • Ellipse ROI with mean ± SD, min / max and area, computed on original images.
05 · MIP & slabs

See the nodule, not the vessel.

In the reading room: maximum intensity projections make small nodules stand out from vessels; minimum intensity projections bring out airways and emphysema.

  • MIP, MinIP and average slabs from 2 to 40 mm, in every plane.
  • Cycle modes with T; adjust thickness with + / −.
  • Measurements always read the original voxels, never the projection.

06 · Comparison

New, stable or growing?

In the reading room: for a follow-up CT, the most important question is how today compares with the prior study.

  • Two studies side by side with the same window.
  • Scrolling linked in millimetres, even when slice spacing differs; zoom and pan mirrored.
  • Align once on a landmark with K, then read both together.
07 · Findings & reports

From viewing to reporting.

In the reading room: every finding needs a name, a value and a location that a colleague or a tumour board can find again.

  • Measurements and markers become named findings, saved automatically.
  • One click jumps back to any finding, in the right plane.
  • A Markdown report draft and annotated key images in PNG.

Get started

Luminor is a small Python server plus a browser app. It runs entirely on your computer.

1 · Install

Python 3.10+ with NumPy and pydicom.

git clone https://github.com/dhnguyends/luminor-dicom.git
cd luminor-dicom
pip install -r requirements.txt
2 · Point it at your scans

A folder with one sub-folder of .dcm files per CT series.

python server.py --data /path/to/series
3 · Read

Open the viewer in Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox, then press ? for shortcuts.

http://127.0.0.1:8770/
